Idaho II
==================================

.. image:: ../../../img/dam-breach/dam-breach0009.png

An analysis was performed in Idaho to evaluate the potential impacts of a structural failure using a physically
explicit modeling approach. In this case, the failure was represented as a prescribed breach in the
containment structure, allowing the simulation to capture the progressive release of stored material
through a defined breach opening.

This approach represents a higher level of modeling detail compared to hydrograph-based methods and is commonly
used when the location and geometry of a potential failure can be reasonably estimated based on
engineering judgment or structural assessment.

The study was conducted to support risk assessment and emergency planning by evaluating how breach development
characteristics influence the timing and magnitude of the released material.

Modeling Objective
---------------------------

The primary objective of this case study was to simulate a structural breach in the dam and evaluate the resulting
release of tailings material as the breach developed over time.

The analysis focused on:

- Defining a realistic breach location
- Simulating progressive breach development
- Evaluating the timing of the release
- Estimating downstream inundation and deposition
- Comparing results with simplified hydrograph methods

This type of modeling approach is commonly used in detailed engineering studies and regulatory evaluations.

Model Setup and Data
---------------------------------

.. image:: ../../../img/dam-breach/dam-breach0010.png

The dam structure was represented as a levee-type containment feature, and the breach was simulated using a
prescribed breach method. The breach location was selected to represent a potential weak point in the structure,
such as a localized deformation zone or erosion-prone section of the embankment.

The model computed the release of water and tailings dynamically as the breach opened and the stored material
flowed through the breach section. Unlike hydrograph-based methods, the discharge was calculated internally by
the model based on the changing geometry of the breach and the hydraulic conditions within the reservoir.

Key modeling steps included:

- Reconstruction of the containment structure
- Identification of the breach element
- Definition of breach geometry and failure direction
- Simulation of breach development over time
- Routing of released material downstream

This method allowed the study to represent the physical interaction between the reservoir,
breach opening, and downstream flow system.

Simulation Results
---------------------------------

.. image:: ../../../img/dam-breach/dam-breach0011.png

The prescribed breach simulation produced a time-dependent release of tailings material that reflected the gradual
development of the breach. The results showed a delayed peak discharge compared to the hydrograph-based method,
followed by sustained flow as the reservoir drained through the breach opening.

The simulation captured the dynamic interaction between breach development and reservoir drawdown,
resulting in a realistic representation of the failure process. Downstream flow patterns were similar to
those observed in the hydrograph-based simulation, but the timing and magnitude of the release differed due
to the physical representation of the breach.

These results demonstrate how breach development assumptions can significantly influence predicted
flood behavior and highlight the importance of selecting an appropriate modeling method for a given project.

Training and Professional Development
------------------------------------------

This case study demonstrates how a prescribed breach can be used to simulate structural failure and evaluate downstream
impacts using a physically based modeling approach. The workflow reflects typical engineering practice for
detailed dam breach analysis and supports decision-making in risk management and emergency planning.

Participants in the associated training program learn how to:

- Define breach geometry and location
- Simulate breach development
- Evaluate failure timing and discharge
- Compare alternative failure scenarios
- Interpret results for risk assessment
