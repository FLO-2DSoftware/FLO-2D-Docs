Idaho I
==================================

.. image:: ../../../img/dam-breach/dam-breach0009.png

A tailings dam located in Idaho, United States, was evaluated to assess the potential downstream impacts associated
with a sudden release of stored tailings material. The objective of the study was to simulate a failure scenario and
estimate the resulting mudflow behavior using the Breach Hydrograph Tool in the FLO-2D model.

The facility represents a typical tailings storage structure constructed within a confined watershed,
where the downstream floodplain includes infrastructure and natural drainage channels that could be affected
by a dam failure. The analysis was conducted as part of a risk assessment exercise to support emergency
planning and consequence evaluation under a conservative failure assumption.

Because the scenario represents a hypothetical failure rather than a historical event, the modeling approach
focused on defining realistic release conditions and evaluating potential runout distances, inundation depths,
and sediment deposition patterns.

Modeling Objective
---------------------------

The primary objective of this case study was to estimate the potential impacts of a tailings dam failure using a
hydrograph-based release method. The simulation was designed to represent a rapid release of stored tailings
material and evaluate the downstream consequences under worst-case conditions.

The analysis focused on:

- Estimating the breach discharge hydrograph
- Simulating the downstream propagation of tailings
- Evaluating potential inundation and deposition zones
- Identifying areas of potential hazard
- Supporting emergency planning and risk communication

This type of modeling approach is commonly used in screening-level hazard assessments and preliminary consequence evaluations.

Model Setup and Data
---------------------------------

.. image:: ../../../img/dam-breach/dam-breach0010.png

In this study, the dam failure was simulated using the Breach Hydrograph Tool, which generates a release hydrograph
based on user-defined reservoir volume, failure mode, and material characteristics. The hydrograph represents the
timing and magnitude of the released material and is applied as an inflow boundary condition in the simulation.

The modeling workflow included:

- Definition of the computational domain
- Assignment of tailings material properties
- Identification of the potential failure location
- Generation of the breach hydrograph
- Estimation of downstream runout extent
- Simulation of downstream flow and deposition

The hydrograph-based method evaluated the consequences of a sudden release event without explicitly s
imulating the progressive erosion of the dam structure.

This approach is particularly useful when:

- Limited structural data are available
- Rapid scenario evaluation is required
- Multiple failure scenarios must be tested
- Conservative impact estimates are needed

Simulation Results
---------------------------------

.. image:: ../../../img/dam-breach/dam-breach0011.png

The simulation predicted the downstream movement of tailings material following a sudden release from the storage facility.
The results showed rapid propagation of the tailings flow along the primary drainage pathway, followed by lateral
spreading across the floodplain as flow velocities decreased.

Areas closest to the dam experienced the highest flow depths and velocities, while downstream areas
exhibited progressive deposition of sediment as the flow energy dissipated.
These patterns are consistent with expected behavior for a tailings release in a confined watershed setting.

The model results provided a clear visualization of potential hazard zones, including:

- Areas subject to high flow depth
- Regions of significant sediment deposition
- Locations where infrastructure could be impacted
- Downstream limits of tailings runout

These outputs support decision-making related to emergency response planning and risk mitigation.

Training and Professional Development
------------------------------------------

This case study demonstrates how the Breach Hydrograph Tool can be used to evaluate hypothetical dam failure scenarios
and communicate potential risks to stakeholders. The workflow is representative of typical engineering practice for
preliminary hazard assessments and emergency planning studies.

Participants learn how to:

- Define a failure scenario
- Generate a breach hydrograph
- Configure tailings flow parameters
- Run stable simulations
- Interpret hazard and inundation results
